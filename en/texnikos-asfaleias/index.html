Why it is needed

The employer is responsible for the health and safety of employees, and the law provides for engaging a safety technician and, where required, an occupational physician (article 498 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 8 of Law 3850/2010). Appointing one does not transfer the responsibility: the safety technician advises and documents, the employer implements (article 533 §§1–3 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 42 of Law 3850/2010). Compliance is inspected by the Labour Inspectorate, and a breach carries administrative and criminal sanctions (articles 540, 542 and 544 of PD 62/2025, formerly articles 69, 71 and 72 of Law 3850/2010).

Which businesses need one

The obligation applies to all businesses in the private and public sectors (articles 492 §1 and 498 of PD 62/2025, formerly articles 2 and 8 of Law 3850/2010); the time required and the technician’s qualifications depend on the business activity (NACE code) and on the number of employees. Typical cases:

  • Workshops and industrial plants with machinery and production lines.
  • Garages, body shops, paint shops and vehicle trade.
  • Manufacturers of bodies, superstructures and trailers.
  • Warehouses and transport companies, with lifting equipment and vehicle movements.
  • Retail shops, food service, offices and services.

Hazard categories

A business falls into one of three categories, based on the NACE code of its activity (article 500 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 10 of Law 3850/2010). The classification table was replaced by article 29 of Law 5239/2025 (Government Gazette A 178), so the category is always checked from the code and not from an impression of the sector.

CategoryHazard level and engagement time
AHighest. The longest engagement time per employee.
BIntermediate. Reduced time compared with category A.
CLowest. The shortest engagement time per employee.

The category, together with the number of employees, also determines the level of knowledge the safety technician must have and whether the employer may take on the duties personally (article 502 §§1–2 and 4–6 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 12 of Law 3850/2010); the specialty is set by the sector of economic activity (article 503 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 13 of Law 3850/2010).

Employer obligations

The employer’s obligations include, among others:

  • Appointing a safety technician and, where required, an occupational physician (articles 498–499 of PD 62/2025, formerly articles 8–9A of Law 3850/2010).
  • Meeting the minimum engagement time that matches the category and the number of employees (article 511 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 21 of Law 3850/2010).
  • The Written Occupational Risk Assessment and a preventive action programme (articles 533–534 of PD 62/2025, formerly articles 42–43 of Law 3850/2010).
  • Acknowledging the safety technician’s recommendations entered in the advice book (article 504 §1 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 14 of Law 3850/2010).
  • Informing and training employees: on hiring, transfer, a change of equipment or technology, and at intervals thereafter (articles 537–538 of PD 62/2025, formerly articles 47–48 of Law 3850/2010).
  • First aid, fire safety, evacuation and measures for serious and imminent danger (article 535 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 45 of Law 3850/2010); an escape and rescue plan (article 520 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 30 of Law 3850/2010).
  • Maintenance of workplaces and regular inspection of safety systems (article 522 §§1–2 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 31 of Law 3850/2010).
  • Reporting occupational accidents (article 534 §2(a) of PD 62/2025, formerly article 43 of Law 3850/2010) and work-related illnesses (article 534 §2(d) of PD 62/2025, added by article 38 of Law 5239/2025, Government Gazette A 178).
  • Consulting and involving employees, and the Health and Safety Committee where provided for (articles 494–497 and 536 of PD 62/2025, formerly articles 4–7 and 46 of Law 3850/2010).

The references are to the Labour Law Code (PD 62/2025, Government Gazette A 121), as amended.

The role of the Safety Technician

A safety technician must hold the required qualifications (article 501 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 11 of Law 3850/2010) and the specialty that matches the activity of the business (article 503 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 13 of Law 3850/2010); we check the specialty from the start, against your NACE code. As safety technician we assess the risks from machinery, lifting equipment, pressure vessels, installations and vehicles, and document our recommendations.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Who provides a safety technician for businesses?

Moudiotis & Associates Technical Office provides safety technician services for businesses in Athens and Thessaloniki. Telephone +30 210 756 1836.

When do I need a safety technician?

The employer must use the services of a safety technician and, in businesses with fifty (50) or more employees, also an occupational physician (article 498 §§1–2 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 8 of Law 3850/2010). The activity (NACE code) and the number of employees determine the category, the minimum engagement time and the qualifications of the safety technician. Contact us and we will tell you what applies to you.

What are the hazard categories?

Three: A (highest), B (intermediate) and C (lowest). The classification comes from the NACE code of the activity (article 500 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 10 of Law 3850/2010).

How many hours of engagement are required?

The minimum time depends on the category and the number of employees (article 511 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 21 of Law 3850/2010). We calculate it for your business as soon as we know the code and the staff.

Can I take on the safety technician duties myself?

For small businesses the law provides a limited possibility, on conditions relating to the category, the number of employees and the employer’s own qualifications (article 502 §§4–6 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 12 of Law 3850/2010). We check whether it applies in your case.

Does appointing a safety technician relieve me of responsibility?

No. Responsibility for the safety of employees remains with the employer (article 533 §§1–3 of PD 62/2025, formerly article 42 of Law 3850/2010). The safety technician advises and documents.

How much does it cost?

Every case is different and may require extensive research. We recommend studying the full file and a separate assessment for your matter.
